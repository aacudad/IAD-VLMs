## §1 Motivation

**¶1 — The inspection problem** ✅ *applied (simplified, dash-free)*
Automated visual inspection is the backbone of modern manufacturing quality assurance. Cameras at the end of an assembly line acquire tens of thousands of high-resolution images per shift. Downstream systems must typically decide, within a fraction of a second, whether the item is up to standard or defective. The two kinds of mistakes are asymmetric. Missing a defect (a false negative) ships a faulty product to the customer, and in safety-critical industries this can trigger a recall that costs far more than the inspection equipment itself. Wrongly rejecting a good part (a false positive) sends it for unnecessary manual rechecking, which slows down the line. For about thirty years, the standard solution was a pipeline of classical computer-vision algorithms that used techniques such as thresholding, template matching and blob analysis, all hand-tuned for each product. In the last five years that pipeline has been replaced by deep neural networks, primarily unsupervised *anomaly detection*: the models are trained only on normal images, and they emit a scalar anomaly score and optionally a localisation heatmap.

**¶2 — MVTec is saturated, three frictions remain** ✅ *applied (simplified, dash-free, millisecond-level, Real-IAD-Variety claim removed)*
On the canonical MVTec AD benchmark [bergmann2019mvtec], PatchCore [roth2022patchcore], EfficientAD [batzner2024efficientad] and SimpleNet [liu2023simplenet] all exceed 99% image-level AUROC at millisecond-level latency. MVTec AD is essentially saturated on pure detection accuracy. Yet in real factories three operational frictions appear repeatedly:

1. **Opacity.** The output is a scalar score and a heatmap. It does not say *what* the defect is, relate the hot region to the object's anatomy ("left side of the bottle neck"), or articulate the comparison to a normal sample. Engineers cannot trust, audit, or write up a result they cannot read.
2. **Per-product training.** Almost every state-of-the-art method needs a per-category training run and a per-category model or memory bank. A line with 30 distinct products (SKUs) maintains 30 detectors and retrains on every tooling, lighting or packaging change.
3. **No few-shot generalisation.** Synthesis-based methods (DRAEM [zavrtanik2021draem], AnomalyDiffusion [hu2024anomalydiffusion]) still need dozens of normal examples; CLIP-based zero-shot (WinCLIP [jeong2023winclip]) plateaus near 90% AUROC with no explanation.

**¶3 — VLMs promise to address all three** *(detailed — unedited; has `--`)*
The recent wave of *vision-language models* (VLMs) `--` BLIP-2 [li2023blip2], LLaVA [liu2023llava], the Qwen-VL line [bai2023qwenvl, wang2024qwen2vl, bai2025qwen25vl], InternVL3 [zhu2025internvl3] `--` promises to address all three frictions simultaneously: they ingest an image and a textual prompt, emit free-form natural language, and have been pre-trained on enough image–text pairs to perform respectably on novel categories in zero shot. A VLM can in principle describe *what*, *where* and *why* an anomaly is present in a single forward pass, and can be steered with a textual instruction rather than a category-specific weight checkpoint.

**¶4 — But not accurate enough yet; prior work** *(detailed — unedited; has `--`)*
The challenge is that out-of-the-box VLMs are far from accurate enough for industrial inspection. On the comprehensive multimodal IAD benchmark MMAD [jiang2025mmad], even GPT-4o reaches only 74.9% average accuracy `--` far below the reliability required for production deployment. Several recent works have begun to close this gap by fine-tuning VLMs on industrial data, either with pure supervised fine-tuning (SFT) (AnomalyGPT [gu2024anomalygpt], Anomaly-OV [xu2025anomalyov]) or with reinforcement-learning post-training (AnomalyR1 [chao2025anomalyr1], LR-IAD [pang2025lriad], EMIT [guan2025emit], IAD-R1 [li2025iadr1]). The latter line applies GRPO [shao2024deepseekmath, deepseekai2025r1] on top of an SFT cold-start to align the model on multi-component rewards. The most directly comparable prior work is IAD-R1 [li2025iadr1], which uses a Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone with SFT+GRPO and is the most recent strong SFT+GRPO open-weight baseline on the MMAD subsets we evaluate; that work establishes the current SOTA against which this thesis compares.

**¶5 — The gap** *(detailed — unedited)*
What is missing in the literature is a controlled, like-for-like investigation of which design choices in the SFT→GRPO pipeline actually drive the gains, and whether the same recipe can be reproduced and improved on commodity (rather than industrial-scale) hardware. This thesis fills that gap.

## §2 Problem Statement and Scope *(detailed — unedited; has `--`)*

**Problem definition.** We address *explainable industrial anomaly detection*: given an image of a manufactured part, output (i) a binary verdict (defect/no defect), (ii) the type of defect if any, (iii) its spatial location, and (iv) a free-text rationale that grounds the verdict in visible evidence. Detection performance is measured by *balanced accuracy* on the MMAD subsets DS-MVTec and VisA, plus a held-out Real-IAD test split. Explanation quality is measured indirectly through per-component reward functions defined over the structured output, and qualitatively through expert review.

**Scope (two restrictions).** First, the backbone family is fixed to Qwen2.5-VL [bai2025qwen25vl]: it is the strongest open-weight VLM at the 3B/7B scale that fits on a single A6000, and the comparable IAD-R1 baseline reports numbers for the same backbone. Second, the training data is restricted to Real-IAD [wang2024realiad] (§2.6), and we do *not* train on any sample drawn from the MMAD benchmark categories used at evaluation time, guaranteeing a clean zero-shot transfer setting. *(✅ deduped: Real-IAD superlative removed → §2.6 ref.)*

## §3 Research Questions

- **RQ1** *(detailed — unedited)* — To what extent does supervised fine-tuning on structured chain-of-thought reasoning traces improve a VLM's balanced accuracy on industrial anomaly detection, and which factors of the trace-generation pipeline (model identity, prompt structure, trace length, automated QA filtering) are responsible for the gain?
- **RQ2** ✅ *applied (dropped the SOTA sub-question, dash-free)* — Does GRPO with a multi-component reward (format, accuracy, semantic-type similarity, spatial location) provide a measurable improvement over the SFT baseline on DS-MVTec and VisA, and does that improvement depend on the strength of the SFT baseline it is applied to?
- **RQ3** *(detailed — unedited)* — Within the controlled SFT→GRPO design space, how do model capacity (3B vs 7B parameters), visual-encoder treatment (frozen vs unfrozen), and training-set size (6K vs 15K traces) trade off against one another, and what does the per-product breakdown reveal about the failure modes that remain?

## §4 Contributions *(detailed prose — unedited except #1's number; has `--`)*

1. **AnomalyThink corpus** ✅ *(exact number applied: 14,472)* — A public-quality reasoning-trace corpus for Real-IAD, **14,472** XML-structured six-phase chain-of-thought traces, product- and class-stratified into a **6,000**-trace SFT pool (AnomalyThink-6K), a **4,236**-trace GRPO pool, and a **4,236**-trace held-out test set (union = AnomalyThink-15K), covering 30 Real-IAD products. To our knowledge the first openly-released reasoning-trace corpus both built on Real-IAD and purpose-designed for GRPO with structured XML output `--` distinct from Anomaly-Instruct-125k [xu2025anomalyov] (conversation-format, industrial subset from MVTec/VisA) and Expert-AD [li2025iadr1] (not public). Prompt template, vocabulary, and generation-time quality controls (auto-reject rules + schema validation, no second model) detailed in Ch.3.
2. **Controlled four-factor SFT study** on Qwen2.5-VL — full fine-tune (no LoRA), 3B/7B × frozen/unfrozen (unfrozen at 15K only) × 6K/15K, up to four epochs. Establishes (a) 7B-frozen-6K reaches **80.16% bal-acc on DS-MVTec**, within ~1.8pp of the released IAD-R1 SFT+GRPO checkpoint *without any GRPO step*; (b) "more data is always better" is wrong here — the curated 6K SFT split beats the full ~15K union by **8.5pp** (80.16 vs 71.66) at matched epochs, a data-count/composition effect not a trace-length one (both ~137-word mean).
3. **GRPO study + honest characterisation of its limits** — two unweighted reward functions (consistency/format + accuracy) → four bounded sub-signals (format, verdict, semantic type, location; max 3.0); KL k3 estimator monitored (β=0, not in loss), G=4, LR 1e-6, 4,236-prompt preference-balanced set. On the original SFT-Iter1 baseline → **82.73 / 70.39 / 80.87** (DS/VisA/held-out Real-IAD), +0.81pp over IAD-R1 on DS under a common harness, on 2×RTX A6000. *However*, this lift is conditioned on a weak SFT baseline: a stronger SFT corpus (rolling out the SFT+GRPO policy, then Gemini correct+rewrite under STaR + Saunders critique-and-revise, 6K) reaches **82.80 / 72.07** with SFT *alone*, and GRPO on top *degrades* it by ~2pp on DS while reward rises; a three-estimator ablation (vanilla z-score, Dr.GRPO, G²RPO) confirms this is independent of the advantage estimator. Best detector = the SFT corpus; single-pass GRPO adds no binary-accuracy headroom on a strong baseline.
4. **Per-product diagnosis** on DS-MVTec — +11.2pp bal-acc of the SFT model over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base; largest gains on previously-difficult products (metal_nut +25.8, cable +19.2, pill +16.0, toothbrush +15.8), only marginal regressions on saturated ones (tile −2.1, wood −0.1).
5. **Discussion of remaining failure modes + roadmap** (Ch.7/Ch.8) — raising VisA accuracy (small/multi-object defects) via higher-resolution inputs and graded spatial rewards, iterative SFT↔RL cycles in the style of OpenVLThinker [deng2025openvlthinker], and an agentic inspection pipeline exposing the VLM to external tools.

## §5 Thesis Outline *(FULL — all 8 entries, previously condensed)*

- **Chapter 2 (Background)** surveys the technical background in three layers: (i) classical industrial AD from PatchCore through EfficientAD, (ii) VLMs from CLIP through Qwen2.5-VL, (iii) RL post-training from PPO through GRPO/GSPO and OpenVLThinker. Ends with a focused review of every published VLM-based IAD method and identifies the gap this thesis fills.
- **Chapter 3 (Dataset & Traces)** describes the reasoning-trace dataset: the six-phase prompt structure, controlled vocabularies for defect type and location, dual-image input format for anomalies, per-product stratified sampling, and generation-time quality controls. Ends with quantitative statistics of the ~14.5K-trace corpus.
- **Chapter 4 (SFT)** presents the supervised fine-tuning methodology: model family, hyperparameters, the LlamaFactory ShareGPT data format, the DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 config with CPU offload, and the four-factor ablation (3B/7B × frozen/unfrozen × 6K/15K × epoch 1–4, unfrozen at 15K only — 6 configurations).
- **Chapter 5 (GRPO)** derives the GRPO objective from first principles, reports the four reward components and their weights, and details the training pipeline (group size, KL coefficient, sample budget) plus production-run statistics from the training log.
- **Chapter 6 (Results)** reports the headline results: aggregate balanced accuracy on DS-MVTec, VisA and held-out Real-IAD; the full per-product breakdown; training/reward dynamics; and a head-to-head with IAD-R1.
- **Chapter 7 (Discussion)** discusses why frozen vision encoders win, why quality dominates quantity, why VisA stays harder than DS-MVTec, and where the remaining errors come from.
- **Chapter 8 (Conclusion)** summarises and lays out next steps: iterative SFT→RL cycles, curriculum sampling, the agentic inspection pipeline.
- **Appendices** — full hyperparameters, the verbatim Gemini 2.5-Flash generation prompt, and per-product evaluation tables.
