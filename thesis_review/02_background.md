**Intro ¶.** This chapter establishes the technical foundations of the thesis and surveys the literature it builds on. §2.1 reviews *classical* industrial anomaly detection (IAD), tracing the four main methodological families from memory banks to real-time student–teacher distillation, and identifies the limitations that motivate the move to language-aware models. §2.2 introduces the vision-language model (VLM) stack on which our system is built, with particular attention to the architecture of Qwen2.5-VL. §2.3 reviews chain-of-thought reasoning and its multimodal extensions, which directly motivate our six-phase trace structure. §2.4 derives the necessary RL background and the GRPO objective. §2.5 surveys the rapidly growing body of work that applies VLMs and RL to industrial anomaly detection, and explicitly positions this thesis relative to the closest published comparison, IAD-R1 [li2025iadr1]. §2.6 introduces the evaluation benchmarks used throughout the rest of the thesis.

## §2.1 Classical Industrial Anomaly Detection

**Framing ¶.** Modern unsupervised IAD methods share three structural assumptions: the training set contains only *nominal* (defect-free) images; defects are rare in test data; and the task is to assign an anomaly score to each image (and, optionally, each pixel) such that defective samples are pushed away from the nominal manifold. Within that framework, four families have come to dominate the MVTec AD leaderboard.

**Memory-bank and feature-embedding methods.** The most influential family is exemplified by **PatchCore** [roth2022patchcore]: a frozen WideResNet-50 extracts patch-level features from every nominal training image, the union of these features is reduced to a representative *coreset* via greedy subsampling, and at inference each test patch is scored by its k-nearest-neighbour distance to that coreset. PatchCore reaches 99.6% image-level AUROC and 98.4% pixel-level AUROC on MVTec AD without any task-specific training of the backbone. The companion approach **PaDiM** [defard2021padim] replaces the non-parametric memory bank with a multivariate Gaussian fitted independently at every spatial location: the test-time anomaly score is the Mahalanobis distance to the fitted normal, which is parallelisable and deterministic. PaDiM reports 97.9% image-AUROC on MVTec AD and remains a standard baseline.

**Normalising-flow methods.** **CFLOW-AD** [gudovskiy2022cflow] pairs a pretrained encoder with conditional normalising-flow decoders `--` one per pyramid level `--` that explicitly model the density of nominal patch features as a unit Gaussian. Conditioning the flow on a positional encoding allows the model to learn that "normal" appearance is location-dependent. The log-likelihood under the trained flow gives a calibrated anomaly map. CFLOW-AD reports 98.3% image-AUROC on MVTec AD at ~10× the throughput of contemporaneous flow-based baselines.

**Reconstruction and distillation methods.** **DRAEM** [zavrtanik2021draem] reformulates IAD as a discriminative problem: a reconstructive sub-network is trained to restore a nominal image from a version corrupted with synthetic Perlin-noise-shaped textures, and a discriminative sub-network is then trained, with pixel-level supervision derived from the synthetic mask, to predict the anomaly segmentation directly. Because the discriminator is trained on labelled (synthetic, but pixel-precise) defects it generalises surprisingly well to real ones (98.0% image-AUROC, 68.4 AU-PRO on MVTec AD). **Reverse Distillation** (RD4AD, [deng2022rd4ad]) instead pairs a frozen pretrained encoder with a learnable decoder that must reconstruct the encoder's multi-scale feature maps from a compact one-class bottleneck embedding; the cosine distance between teacher and student features on anomalous inputs supplies the score (98.5% image-AUROC, 97.8% pixel-AUROC). **EfficientAD** [batzner2024efficientad] pushes the latency envelope: a four-layer Patch-Description-Network student-teacher pair, combined with a lightweight autoencoder branch that handles logical anomalies, reaches >99% mean AUROC on 32 datasets at ~2 ms inference time and 600 FPS on commodity GPUs. EfficientAD is the canonical efficiency-oriented baseline against which any VLM-based method must justify its higher computational cost.

**Self-supervised and synthetic-defect methods.** **CutPaste** [li2021cutpaste] introduces a self-supervised pretext task `--` cut a patch from a nominal image and paste it at a random location `--` and trains a CNN as a three-way classifier (normal, CutPaste, CutPaste-scar). The resulting representation, augmented with a Gaussian density estimator, reaches 96.6% image-AUROC on MVTec AD without any defective training data. **SimpleNet** [liu2023simplenet] extends this idea to the feature space: a frozen extractor, a shallow Feature Adaptor, and an Anomaly Feature Generator that adds Gaussian perturbations to nominal feature vectors together feed a small binary Discriminator. SimpleNet reaches 99.6% image-AUROC on MVTec AD at 77 FPS. Most recently, **AnomalyDiffusion** [hu2024anomalydiffusion] leverages text-to-image diffusion to *synthesise* realistic defect images from a handful of real exemplars, with a Spatial Anomaly Embedding that disentangles defect appearance from spatial location and an attention re-weighting mechanism that forces the diffusion model to attend to under-generated regions.

**Limitations of classical IAD methods.** On MVTec AD all four families saturate near 99% image-AUROC and the benchmark no longer discriminates between methods. Five operational limitations remain, however, and motivate the move to VLMs.
1. *No language explanation.* Every classical method outputs a scalar and a heatmap; none of them can articulate "a 3 mm horizontal scratch near the upper-right edge of the threaded cylinder". This is the explanation gap addressed in this thesis.
2. *Per-category training and per-category memory.* Each of PatchCore, PaDiM, CFLOW-AD, DRAEM, RD4AD, EfficientAD and SimpleNet requires a separate training run, or at least a separate coreset/memory bank, per object category. Real-IAD Variety [zhu2025realiadvariety] showed that scaling these methods from 30 to 160 categories drops mean AUROC by 10–20 points. *(✅ verified against the paper.)*
3. *Saturation on structural-only benchmarks.* Image-level AUROC >99% on MVTec AD masks the fact that classical methods struggle with *logical* anomalies (wrong-number-of-pushpins, swapped components) introduced in MVTec LOCO [bergmann2022mvtecloco], where they regress by 10–30 points.
4. *Limited few-shot generalisation.* Synthesis-based methods need a handful of real defects to bootstrap their pseudo-defects, and even then do not generalise across SKUs without re-tuning.
5. *No textual steerability.* An operator cannot ask a PatchCore to "ignore the company logo printed on the cap"; the only control surface is the training set.

> **[takeaway box]** Classical IAD has solved *detection* on canonical benchmarks but not *explanation*, *per-product scalability*, or *instructional steerability*. These are precisely the capabilities VLMs make natively available.

## §2.2 Vision-Language Models

**Framing ¶.** A vision-language model maps a pair (I, x) of an image I and a text prompt x to a sequence of text tokens. Modern VLMs share a three-component recipe: (i) a pretrained image encoder that turns I into a sequence of visual tokens; (ii) a connector that projects those visual tokens into the embedding space of a pretrained language model (LM); and (iii) the LM itself, which autoregressively generates the response conditioned on the concatenation of visual and text tokens.

**From CLIP to instruction-tuned VLMs.** The first system to establish the textual-supervision paradigm at internet scale was **CLIP** [radford2021clip], a dual-encoder model trained on 400M (image, text) pairs by maximising the cosine similarity of matched pairs and minimising it for the N²−N in-batch negatives. CLIP performs no generation but provides a strong zero-shot classification interface and an image encoder that has since been reused throughout the VLM stack. **BLIP-2** [li2023blip2] introduced the now-canonical recipe: a frozen ViT image encoder is connected to a frozen LLM through a small Q-Former that contains a fixed set of learnable query tokens that cross-attend to image features. **LLaVA** [liu2023llava] simplified the connector to a single linear projection, and performed the first *visual instruction tuning* by using GPT-4 to synthesise 158K multimodal instruction-following examples; the resulting model reaches 92.5% of GPT-4's performance on ScienceQA. **LLaVA-OneVision** [li2024llavaonevision] extended the recipe to a unified single-image / multi-image / video model.

**The Qwen-VL family.** The Qwen line [bai2023qwenvl, wang2024qwen2vl, bai2025qwen25vl] provides the backbone for this thesis. **Qwen-VL** introduced a position-aware adapter that compresses image features to 256 tokens; **Qwen2-VL** [wang2024qwen2vl] added two changes critical for industrial inspection: *Naive Dynamic Resolution*, which encodes images at native resolution and yields a variable number of visual tokens, and *Multimodal Rotary Position Embedding* (M-RoPE), which decomposes positional encoding into temporal/height/width components so that text, image and video share a single position space. **Qwen2.5-VL** [bai2025qwen25vl] extends Qwen2-VL with: a redesigned ViT using *window attention* for efficiency, absolute time encoding for long video, improved object grounding (bounding boxes and points), and stronger document parsing. We use the 3B and 7B variants throughout this thesis. Qwen2.5-VL's native dynamic-resolution behaviour is particularly important for IAD because industrial defects can occur at arbitrary scales, ranging from sub-millimetre scratches to fully missing components.

The closest open-weight alternative is **InternVL3** [zhu2025internvl3], which uses a *native multimodal* pretraining paradigm and Variable Visual Position Encoding (V2PE). On general multimodal benchmarks InternVL3-78B reaches 72.2 on MMMU, the highest open-source score at release time. Proprietary baselines we cite include **GPT-4V** [openai2023gpt4v] and Google's **Gemini 2.5** [geminiteam2025gemini25]. Gemini 2.5-Flash is the trace-generation model used in Chapter 3.

## §2.3 Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

**Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting** [wei2022cot] is the technique of supplying a few exemplars of intermediate reasoning steps, so that a sufficiently large LM decomposes a problem into a chain of textual sub-claims before emitting a final answer. CoT improves arithmetic, commonsense and symbolic reasoning, and emerges only beyond ~100B parameters. **Self-Consistency** [wang2023selfconsistency] samples N chains under temperature decoding and marginalises over the final answer, yielding double-digit gains on GSM8K and other reasoning benchmarks. **Tree of Thoughts** [yao2023tot] generalises the linear chain to a search tree over thoughts and traverses it with BFS/DFS plus self-evaluation, lifting Game-of-24 accuracy from 4% to 74%.

For visual tasks, **Multimodal-CoT** [zhang2023multimodalcot] introduced a two-stage architecture that first generates a textual rationale conditioned on image and question, then conditions answer inference on image, question and rationale. The central finding is that grounding the rationale in visual features mitigates hallucination `--` a property we rely on in our six-phase trace template. This thesis pushes the idea one step further by enforcing a *structured* chain-of-thought with a fixed XML schema (`<think>`, `<location>`, `<type>`, `<answer>`), which has three practical advantages: (i) the structure is machine-checkable, enabling format rewards in RL; (ii) it forces evidence-before-conclusion, which we measure empirically reduces hallucinated defects; and (iii) it makes downstream parsing trivial.

## §2.4 Reinforcement Learning for Language Models

**Framing ¶.** The RL background relevant to this thesis is the alignment of an already-pretrained policy π_θ with a scalar reward signal r(·). We summarise the relevant algorithms in chronological order and then derive GRPO in the form actually used in Chapter 5.

**From PPO to RLHF.** **Proximal Policy Optimisation** (PPO, [schulman2017ppo]) maximises a clipped surrogate **[Eq 2.1: L^CLIP = E_t[min(r_t·Â_t, clip(r_t,1−ε,1+ε)·Â_t)]]**, where r_t(θ) is the importance ratio and Â_t is the generalised advantage estimate, requiring a learned value function. PPO became the workhorse of language-model alignment via the *Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback* (RLHF) pipeline introduced by [ouyang2022instructgpt]: a reference policy is first SFT'd on demonstrations, a reward model is fitted on pairwise human preferences with a Bradley–Terry loss, and the SFT policy is then fine-tuned against the reward model with PPO and a KL penalty to the reference. **Direct Preference Optimisation** (DPO, [rafailov2023dpo]) showed that the same KL-constrained optimum can be reached without explicit reward modelling, by reparameterising the reward in terms of the policy: a simple binary cross-entropy loss on preferred/dispreferred completions suffices.

**Group Relative Policy Optimisation (GRPO).** **GRPO** was introduced as part of DeepSeekMath [shao2024deepseekmath] and popularised for reasoning by DeepSeek-R1 [deepseekai2025r1]. Its central simplification is to *replace the learned value function* of PPO with a Monte-Carlo group baseline: for each prompt q, sample a group of G completions {o_1,…,o_G} from the old policy π_θold, score each with the reward function to obtain {r_1,…,r_G}, and compute a group-relative advantage **[Eq 2.2: Â_{i,t} = (r_i − mean({r}))/std({r})]**, which is shared by every token t of the trajectory o_i. The GRPO surrogate is then **[Eq 2.3: J_GRPO = E[ (1/G)Σ_i (1/|o_i|)Σ_t { min(ρ_{i,t}Â_{i,t}, clip(ρ,1−ε,1+ε)Â_{i,t}) − β·KL[π_θ‖π_ref] } ]]** with token-level ratio ρ_{i,t}(θ). The KL term, when used, is placed inside the loss (weighted by β) and computed via Schulman's unbiased k₃ estimator [schulman2020klestimator]; **in this thesis we set β=0, so it is monitored but not added to the loss** (see Chapter 5): **[Eq 2.4: KL ≈ (π_ref/π_θ) − log(π_ref/π_θ) − 1]**, which is always non-negative. The hyperparameters governing the algorithm are G (group size), ε (clipping range), and β (KL coefficient).

GRPO is attractive for VLM-IAD for three reasons. (i) The verifier-style reward is sparse and binary-ish (correct/incorrect verdict), exactly the regime PPO's critic struggles with. (ii) The group baseline saves the entire value-network memory cost, which is significant on a 7B model. (iii) The reward function can be *decomposed* into structured components (format, accuracy, type, location) and summed without changing the algorithm `--` a property we exploit in Chapter 5.

The Qwen team's follow-up **GSPO** [zheng2025gspo] replaces GRPO's token-level importance ratio with a sequence-level ratio for stability at MoE scale; we discuss it in Chapter 8 as a candidate for future work but do not use it here. Related critic-free algorithms include RLOO [ahmadian2024rloo] and REINFORCE++ [hu2025reinforceplusplus]. The most directly relevant prior work to our SFT→GRPO recipe is **OpenVLThinker** [deng2025openvlthinker], which showed that iterative SFT↔RL cycles outperform single-round SFT+RL on multimodal reasoning `--` a roadmap item we instantiate as an IAD-specific ablation in §grpo-iter2 and to which we return in §conclusion-future. Its sequel **OpenVLThinker v2** [hu2026openvlthinkerv2] introduces **G²RPO**, a non-parametric replacement for the z-score advantage of [Eq 2.2] that maps within-group reward ranks onto the inverse standard-normal CDF; we evaluate this variant on IAD in §grpo-g2rpo.

## §2.5 VLM-Based Industrial Anomaly Detection

VLM-based IAD has crystallised in the last two years along three paradigms: *zero-shot CLIP-based* methods, *supervised fine-tuning* methods, and *SFT+RL* methods.

**Zero-shot CLIP-based methods.** **WinCLIP** [jeong2023winclip] is the canonical zero-shot CLIP-based detector: a compositional prompt ensemble over state words ("normal", "damaged") and template strings is paired with a multi-window aggregation of CLIP features to yield patch/window/image-level anomaly scores. It reaches 91.8% image-AUROC on MVTec AD without any training. **AnomalyCLIP** [zhou2024anomalyclip] learns two *object-agnostic* prompt templates shared across all categories, using auxiliary annotated data; the resulting prompts transfer better across domains than category-specific ones. **PromptAD** [li2024promptad] targets the few-shot setting by appending defect suffixes to learned normal prompts (*semantic concatenation*), and **AdaCLIP** [cao2024adaclip] introduces hybrid (static + dynamic) prompts. All of these methods operate on frozen CLIP and produce only a score plus an attention map `--` no language-level explanation.

**Supervised fine-tuning methods.** **AnomalyGPT** [gu2024anomalygpt] is the earliest LLaVA-based industrial AD VLM: a lightweight pixel decoder is added to LLaVA, and learnable prompt embeddings (rather than full LLM weights) are tuned on simulated and few real defects. With one normal reference shot, it reaches 86.1% accuracy and 94.1% image-AUROC on MVTec AD. **Anomaly-OV** [xu2025anomalyov] is built on LLaVA-OneVision and introduces a Look-Twice Feature Matching mechanism that re-attends to suspicious regions, together with the Anomaly-Instruct-125K instruction-tuning corpus and the VisA-D&R detection-plus-reasoning benchmark. These methods do not use RL.

**SFT+RL methods.** **AnomalyR1** [chao2025anomalyr1] is the first end-to-end MLLM IAD method to use GRPO. The base model is Qwen2.5-VL-3B, optimised with a single Reasoned Outcome Alignment Metric (ROAM) reward that jointly scores reasoning fidelity and answer/localisation precision. **LR-IAD** [pang2025lriad] uses Qwen2-VL-7B and a focal-loss-inspired reward to combat class imbalance, with no pixel-level masks. **EMIT** [guan2025emit] uses InternVL3-8B and *difficulty-aware* GRPO: when no rollout in a group is correct, additional responses are re-sampled so the policy never sees a zero-signal batch. **AD-FM** [adfm2025] adds a 're-think' stage and uses GIoU rewards for bounding-box localisation.

The most relevant comparison for this thesis is **IAD-R1** [li2025iadr1], the current strongest publicly-released SFT+GRPO open-weight baseline. Its pipeline has two stages: PA-SFT (Perception Activation SFT) on a curated 2.9K-sample subset of their *Expert-AD* corpus, followed by SC-GRPO (Structured Control GRPO) on the remaining 3K samples with four rewards: R_acc (binary verdict), R_loc (3×3 grid match), R_type (graded type similarity), R_con (format consistency). IAD-R1 is evaluated by its authors zero-shot on six datasets (MVTec, MPDD, VisA, DAGM, DTD, SDD); the LLaVA-OneVision-7B backbone is the headline of that work. We do not quote the IAD-R1 paper's own evaluation numbers in this thesis; for all comparisons against IAD-R1 we instead re-run the publicly released Qwen2.5-VL-7B IAD-R1 checkpoint through the same evaluation harness used for our own models, so that the comparison is on a strictly common footing (see §res-sota).

> **[takeaway box]** The closest prior work to this thesis is IAD-R1, which combines SFT and GRPO on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and is the current strongest publicly-released SFT+GRPO baseline on the MMAD DS-MVTec / VisA subsets. Our experimental contribution will be to (a) match-or-beat IAD-R1 with structured six-phase traces under a common evaluation harness, (b) test whether a four-component GRPO with a semantic type reward improves further over the SFT baseline `--` and under what conditions it does not, and (c) provide the first like-for-like ablation across 3B/7B, frozen/unfrozen, 6K/15K conditions.

## §2.6 Benchmark Datasets

This thesis uses two datasets: Real-IAD for training, with a held-out split reserved for out-of-distribution evaluation, and MMAD (specifically the DS-MVTec and VisA subsets) for primary evaluation. *(✅ applied "two datasets" reframe, commit `5185f29`; held-out kept since its 80.87% is reported in abstract/intro/Ch.6.)*

**Real-IAD** [wang2024realiad] contains 150K+ high-resolution images across 30 product categories captured from five viewpoints, with ~50K anomalous samples and pixel-precise masks. It is roughly an order of magnitude larger than MVTec AD and re-introduces meaningful headroom over classical methods (mean AUROC drops from >99% on MVTec to ~85% on Real-IAD for state-of-the-art methods). Real-IAD Variety [zhu2025realiadvariety] extends this to 160 categories across 28 industries; we use it as a stress test only.

**MVTec AD** [bergmann2019mvtec] is the de facto standard for unsupervised IAD: 5,354 images of 15 categories, 70+ defect types, pixel-precise masks. **VisA** [zou2022visa] contains 10,821 images of 12 multi-object scenes (PCBs, capsules, snacks) and is generally harder for classical detectors because it requires reasoning about object-level structure rather than only surface texture.

**MMAD** [jiang2025mmad] is the first comprehensive VLM benchmark for IAD. It defines seven subtasks (anomaly discrimination, defect classification/localisation/description/analysis, object classification/structure) and provides 39,672 questions over 8,366 images drawn from MVTec AD, MVTec LOCO, VisA, GoodsAD and the Defect Spectrum re-annotation of MVTec. Even GPT-4o tops out at 74.9% average accuracy, far below industrial deployment requirements. We use the DS-MVTec subset of MMAD (1,670 samples) and the VisA subset (2,141 samples) as our primary evaluation pair, consistent with IAD-R1's reporting.

**What DS-MVTec is.** The DS-MVTec subset is the *Defect Spectrum* re-annotation [yang2024defectspectrum] of the 15 MVTec AD categories (hence the "DS" prefix), repackaged by MMAD as multiple-choice question answering. It is therefore distinct from the original MVTec AD benchmark above in two ways. First, its images carry finer-grained, semantically richer defect labels. Second, and more importantly for this thesis, we evaluate it in the MMAD question-answering setting, scoring the binary anomaly-detection verdict with balanced accuracy, rather than in the original unsupervised image-level AUROC setting. The near-perfect AUROC figures quoted for MVTec AD and the balanced-accuracy figures we report on DS-MVTec are therefore measured under different protocols and not directly comparable.
